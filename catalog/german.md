### Duden online

*The authority on German spelling and usage — monolingual definitions, free online.*

dictionary, website, tool · B1–C2 · Freemium · vocabulary, writing, grammar

Duden is what settles arguments about German spelling and grammar, and the online dictionary gives you definitions in German with declension tables, register labels, synonyms and etymology. Switching to it from a bilingual dictionary is a real milestone around B1. Core lookup is free; the Duden-Mentor writing corrector and some premium content are paid.

[Website](https://www.duden.de/)

### dict.cc

*Community-built German-English dictionary that knows idioms and regional usage.*

dictionary, website, app · A1–C2 · Free · vocabulary, reading, writing

Nearly 40,000 volunteer contributors have made this the go-to German-English dictionary for exactly the phrases Google Translate mangles — compounds, idioms, technical terms, Austrian and Swiss variants. Every entry shows gender, plural and usage tags, and there's an offline mobile app. Free; the crowdsourced nature occasionally shows in odd or unverified entries, so cross-check anything surprising.

[Website](https://www.dict.cc/) · [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dict-cc-dictionary/id327732352) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=cc.dict.dictcc)

### DW Learn German

*Germany's public broadcaster gives away a full A1–C1 German curriculum, free.*

course, website, media · A1–C1 · Free · listening, reading, grammar, vocabulary

Deutsche Welle publishes complete graded courses, placement tests, vocabulary trainers, Deutschtrainer clips and news formats like Top-Thema — all free, no ads, no upsell, available in dozens of interface languages. In terms of value per euro nothing else in German learning is close. The interface is dense and it's easy to wander rather than follow a path, so pick one course track and stick to it.

[Website](https://learngerman.dw.com/)

### Easy German (YouTube)

*Street interviews with real Germans, dual German/English subtitles on every video.*

youtube · A1–C1 · Freemium · listening, vocabulary, immersion

The flagship of the genre: unscripted interviews at native speed plus 'Super Easy German' episodes where the hosts slow down for beginners, all dual-subtitled so the same video works at several levels. It's the fastest cure for the shock of real German after textbook audio. Videos are free; membership adds interactive transcripts, exercises and a Discord community.

[Website](https://www.youtube.com/@EasyGerman)

### Goethe-Institut German courses

*Official German courses and certificates from Germany's state cultural institute.*

course, tutoring, community · A1–C2 · Paid · speaking, listening, writing, grammar

The Goethe-Institut runs in-person and online German courses worldwide and issues the Goethe-Zertifikat, the certificate most widely recognised by German employers, universities and immigration authorities. Teaching is professionally qualified and the exam pathway is unambiguous. It is among the most expensive options, and availability and scheduling vary a lot by country — check your local institute rather than the global site.

[Website](https://www.goethe.de/en/spr/kup.html)

### LEO German-English Dictionary

*Long-established German dictionary whose forums answer the questions dictionaries can't.*

dictionary, website, community · A1–C2 · Free · vocabulary, reading, writing

LEO's real advantage over dict.cc is the discussion forum attached to entries: for any ambiguous word there's usually a thread where native speakers argue out the nuance, which is often more useful than the entry itself. Includes conjugation and declension tables and audio pronunciation. Free and ad-supported, with an interface that hasn't changed much in years.

[Website](https://dict.leo.org/)

### Goethe-Institut — Deutsch üben

### Menschen (Hueber)

### YourDailyGerman

### German with Laura

### Hammer's German Grammar and Usage

*The definitive English-language reference grammar of German, for when you need the real rule.*

book · B2–C2 · Paid · grammar, writing, reading

Martin Durrell's Hammer is what university German departments use: exhaustive, precise, with attested examples and explicit notes on register and regional variation. It answers the edge cases every learner grammar waves away. It is a reference, not a course — dense, expensive, and useless for practice, so buy it only once you're past B1 and the shallow explanations have started failing you.
