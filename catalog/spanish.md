### SpanishDict

*The default English–Spanish dictionary, with the best free conjugation tables online.*

dictionary, website, tool · Freemium · vocabulary, grammar, reading

Every entry gives multiple translations with example sentences, regional labels and audio from both Spain and Latin America, and the conjugator covers every tense of any verb you type. The free tier is genuinely enough for daily lookups; Premium mostly adds ad removal and extra lessons. Its own grammar articles are clear but shallow — fine for a quick answer, not a substitute for a real course.

[Website](https://www.spanishdict.com/)

### Butterfly Spanish

*Ana's warm, blackboard-style grammar explanations — the biggest Spanish teaching channel.*

youtube · A1–B1 · Free · grammar, vocabulary, listening

With around 1.6 million subscribers, this is the channel most beginners land on, and it deserves the reach: Ana explains one grammar point or vocabulary set per video, in English early on and increasingly in Mexican Spanish later. Uploads have slowed to roughly one every month or two, but the back catalogue is a complete beginner grammar course in itself. Best used as targeted explanation when something confuses you, not as a curriculum to work through in order.

[Website](https://www.youtube.com/@ButterflySpanish)

### Coffee Break Spanish

*Four seasons of 15-minute lessons taking you from absolute beginner to advanced.*

podcast, course · A1–C1 · Freemium · listening, grammar, vocabulary, speaking

Mark teaches while a learner-stand-in makes the mistakes you would make, which is a well-worn format executed unusually well and in a genuinely structured sequence. All four seasons of audio are free in any podcast app; the paid versions (around £199 per season) add video lessons, bonus audio and lesson notes. Scottish-accented English explanation with Spanish from Spain — the free audio alone is a complete beginner course.

[Website](https://coffeebreaklanguages.com/coffeebreakspanish/) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/coffee-break-spanish/id201598403) · [RSS](https://feeds.acast.com/public/shows/985e7c00-8945-4e0d-a4da-b93049180ce1)

### Diccionario de la lengua española (RAE)

*The official monolingual Spanish dictionary from the RAE and the 22 language academies.*

dictionary · Free · vocabulary, reading

The authority on what a Spanish word means, including regional labels showing which country a sense belongs to — the thing to reach for when a bilingual dictionary gives you three English words and no way to choose. Free, ad-free and continuously updated online. Definitions are written for native speakers in dense academic Spanish, so it is not a beginner tool; use SpanishDict until monolingual definitions stop being harder than the word itself.

[Website](https://dle.rae.es/)

### Dreaming Spanish

*Thousands of hours of levelled comprehensible-input video, from zero to advanced.*

website, course, media · A0–C1 · Freemium · listening, immersion, vocabulary

The reference implementation of the comprehensible-input method: nine native teachers from different countries make videos graded from Superbeginner to Advanced, with a roadmap measured in hours watched rather than lessons finished. Premium is about $8/month, but hundreds of hours sit free on the YouTube channel, so you can test the method before paying. It deliberately delays speaking and teaches no explicit grammar — pair it with a tutor or a grammar reference if you need to produce Spanish soon. Note the site moved from dreamingspanish.com to dreaming.com/spanish when they added French.

[Website](https://www.dreaming.com/spanish)

### Duolingo Spanish Podcast

*True Latin American stories told in slow Spanish, framed by an English narrator.*

podcast · A2–B2 · Free · listening, vocabulary

Each episode follows one real person's story, told in clear intermediate Spanish with an English host stitching the scenes together, so you never lose the thread even when you lose a sentence. Production quality is excellent and transcripts are free on the site. It stopped publishing after episode 151 in early 2024, but the roughly 150-episode archive remains one of the best free bridges from beginner audio to real speech.

[Website](https://podcast.duolingo.com/spanish) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/duolingo-spanish-podcast/id1317493077) · [RSS](https://anchor.fm/s/fc714074/podcast/rss) · [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/show/2uDEXRSkpRdCmZUw8qt5fh)

### Easy Spanish (YouTube)

*Unscripted street interviews across Spain and Latin America, dual-subtitled.*

youtube · B1–C1 · Free · listening, immersion, vocabulary

Ordinary people answering questions on the street, at full natural speed, with Spanish and English subtitles burned in — the closest free thing to eavesdropping. Uploads are frequent and the channel spans Mexico, Spain, Argentina and more, so you hear real accent variety rather than one teacher's voice. The unedited speed and slang make it hard before B1; the membership tier adds transcripts and vocabulary sheets.

### Destinos: An Introduction to Spanish

*52-episode telenovela built as a full Spanish course, free with textbooks and workbooks.*

course, media · A1–B1 · Free · listening, vocabulary, grammar

A 1990s Annenberg production in which a lawyer traces a family mystery across Spain, Argentina, Puerto Rico and Mexico, deliberately exposing you to each country's accent while the language difficulty ramps up episode by episode. Annenberg's learner.org shut down in July 2026; this independent non-profit archive now hosts all 52 episodes plus the textbooks, workbooks and audio, free. The production values are unmistakably three decades old, but as a free structured beginner course with real narrative pull it has no modern equal.

[Website](https://destinostelenovela.org/)

### Español con Juan

### Linguriosa

### Radio Ambulante

*Long-form Latin American narrative journalism — native-speed Spanish worth listening to.*

podcast, media · B2–C2 · Free · listening, immersion, vocabulary

This is not a learner podcast: it is award-winning audio journalism made for Spanish speakers, telling one deeply reported Latin American story per episode. That is exactly why it works as a graduation target — accents rotate across the whole continent and nothing is slowed down for you. Free with full transcripts on the site; if you can follow an episode unaided you are comfortably B2+. The Jiveworld app was built specifically to make these episodes tractable earlier.

### Short Stories in Spanish for Beginners (Olly Richards)

*Eight genre short stories written inside the 1,000 most frequent Spanish words.*

book · A2–B1 · Paid · reading, vocabulary

Proper graded reading: sci-fi, crime and thriller stories with bolded vocabulary, chapter glossaries, plot summaries and comprehension questions, so you can read a whole book without a dictionary at your elbow. It is the standard recommendation for the moment textbook dialogues stop being enough, and there is a Volume 2 plus an intermediate edition when you outgrow it. Published by Teach Yourself; the audiobook is sold separately, which is an annoying split if you want to read and listen together.

[Website](https://us.teachyourself.com/products/short-stories-in-spanish-for-beginners)

### StudySpanish.com

*A plain, complete free grammar and pronunciation reference with quizzes on every topic.*

website, course · A1–B1 · Freemium · grammar, pronunciation, vocabulary, writing

An old-school site with a full grammar tutorial, a 50-lesson pronunciation tutorial and verb drills, all free and all with tests you can record grades on with a free account. The design is dated and there is nothing gamified about it, which is exactly why it is still useful — you can look up ser vs estar or the subjunctive and get a straight answer. ### Centro Virtual Cervantes

website · B1–C2 · Free · reading, vocabulary, grammar, immersion

The Spanish government's own language institute publishes a large free library here — the Rayuela interactive exercise bank, the daily Rinconete culture column, translation and neologism blogs, and teaching materials. ### Jiveworld (formerly Lupa)

*Turns real Radio Ambulante episodes into a structured listening workout.*

app · B1–C1 · Paid · listening, reading, vocabulary

An 'ears first' app that walks you through an authentic podcast episode in passes: listen blind, then with hints, then with full text, with speed control and vocabulary support at each stage. It is the most effective bridge from learner audio to native audio that exists, and the iOS/Android apps are well built. It is expensive for what is essentially one skill, and it only makes sense once you are around B1 — below that the source material is hopeless. Launched as Lupa and renamed Jiveworld.

[Website](https://www.jiveworld.com/) · [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spanish-fluency-for-real-life/id1480744663) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.jiveworld.lupa) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@jiveworldapp)

### Kwiziq Spanish

*Adaptive grammar drilling that maps exactly what you know against the CEFR levels.*

course, tool · A1–C1 · Freemium · grammar, writing, reading

You take a placement test, and from then on it feeds you micro-quizzes on the specific grammar points you keep missing, tracking progress on a per-topic 'brainmap'. It is the best tool available for closing grammar gaps systematically, and the lesson notes behind each point are unusually well written. The free tier limits how many quizzes you get per month, and it does nothing for listening or speaking — treat it as the grammar half of a wider plan.

[Website](https://spanish.kwiziq.com/)
