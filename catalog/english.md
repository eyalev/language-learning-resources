### Cambridge Dictionary

*Learner definitions plus bilingual entries in dozens of languages, all free.*

dictionary · A1–C2 · Free · vocabulary, reading, pronunciation

The other great free learner's dictionary, distinguished by pairing every English entry with a translation into your own language — genuinely useful when a monolingual definition leaves you unsure — plus CEFR levels, example sentences, both accents in audio and a good grammar section. It is often quicker than Oxford for a rough sense of a word. Coverage of newer slang lags, the bilingual entries are much thinner than the English ones for smaller languages, and the site is ad-heavy.

[Website](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/)

### English Grammar in Use (Murphy)

*The default intermediate grammar book: one point per two-page spread.*

book · B1–B2 · Paid · grammar, writing

Raymond Murphy's blue book has taught grammar to tens of millions of learners with a simple format — explanation and examples on the left page, exercises on the right — covering everything an intermediate learner needs, with an answer key that makes it genuinely self-study. The fifth edition adds an interactive ebook with audio. Companion volumes exist for elementary (red) and advanced (green) levels, so buy the one that matches you; and it is a practice book, not a course, with nothing on listening, speaking or vocabulary building.

[Website](https://www.cambridge.org/us/cambridgeenglish/catalog/grammar-vocabulary-and-pronunciation/english-grammar-use-5th-edition)

### 6 Minute English

*Weekly six-minute discussion built around six target words, with transcript.*

podcast · B1–C1 · Free · listening, vocabulary

Two presenters discuss a topic — sleep science, AI, urban foxes — at a controlled pace, teaching six pieces of vocabulary along the way, with a full transcript and PDF for every episode. Short enough to do daily on a commute and consistent enough that it becomes a habit, which is why it is the most widely recommended English podcast for learners. The conversational format is scripted rather than spontaneous, so it will not prepare you for fast native speech on its own, and below about B1 the pace is still demanding.

[Website](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish/features/6-minute-english) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/6-minute-english/id262026947) · [RSS](https://podcasts.files.bbci.co.uk/p02pc9tn.rss)

### BBC Learning English

*The biggest free English learning site, updated daily by the BBC.*

website, course, media · A1–C1 · Free · listening, vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation

Decades of programmes organised into courses by level, plus daily news-based vocabulary, pronunciation videos covering every English phoneme, grammar explainers and drama series, all with transcripts and all free with no account required. It is the single most reliable free starting point for a learner of British English and it was still publishing new material in August 2026. The volume is its weakness as well as its strength — the site is a sprawl of overlapping series, so pick one course and follow it rather than browsing at random, and note the model is British throughout.

[Website](https://www.bbc.co.uk/learningenglish)

### British Council LearnEnglish

*Free skills practice tagged by CEFR level, from the British Council.*

website, course · A1–C1 · Free · reading, listening, grammar, writing, vocabulary

Every activity is labelled by level and skill — reading texts, listening clips, grammar explanations, writing models for emails and essays — with comprehension exercises attached, and all of it is free without an account. Its structure by CEFR level makes it far easier to navigate than most free sites, and there is a free level test to place yourself. The material is exam-and-classroom flavoured rather than entertaining, and the British Council's genuinely paid products (English Online courses, IELTS prep) are marketed alongside it, so check what you are clicking.

[Website](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/) · [YouTube](https://youtube.com/@BritishCouncilEnglish)

### English with Lucy

*Polished British-English lessons on pronunciation, idiom and common mistakes.*

youtube, course · A2–C1 · Freemium · pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar

Lucy Bella Earl covers Received Pronunciation, British idioms, confusable words and grammar traps in well-produced videos aimed at intermediate learners, and the channel was still posting weekly in August 2026 to an audience in the millions. It is the most accessible entry point for learners specifically targeting British English. The free videos are broad rather than systematic and increasingly funnel towards the paid courses and study packs, so treat them as supplements to a structured programme rather than the programme itself.

[Website](https://englishwithlucy.com/) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@EnglishwithLucy)

### Oxford Learner's Dictionaries

*Definitions written in a 3,000-word vocabulary, with CEFR level on every word.*

dictionary · A1–C2 · Free · vocabulary, reading, pronunciation, writing

Definitions are constrained to a controlled defining vocabulary, so you never need a second dictionary to understand the first one, and each headword carries a CEFR tag telling you whether it is worth learning yet, alongside British and American audio, grammar patterns and collocations. The free site covers the full Advanced Learner's Dictionary. It is monolingual by design, which is right for intermediate learners and off-putting for absolute beginners, and the ads and app upsells on the free site are noticeable.

[Website](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/)

### Simple English Wikipedia

*Wikipedia rewritten in short sentences and a basic vocabulary, free.*

website, media · A2–B2 · Free · reading, vocabulary

Around a quarter of a million articles written with simple words and short sentences, covering the same subjects as full Wikipedia — an endless supply of free reading on whatever you are already interested in, which is the hardest thing to find at lower levels. Reading about your own field here first makes the full English article much easier afterwards. Coverage is uneven and articles are much shorter and sometimes less accurate or less current than the main edition, so treat it as reading practice rather than a reference.

[Website](https://simple.wikipedia.org/)

### VOA Learning English

*Daily American news read at reduced speed with a controlled vocabulary.*

media, website · A2–B2 · Free · listening, reading, vocabulary

News, science and culture stories written with a limited word list and read at about two-thirds of normal speed, each with the full text alongside the audio, plus long-running series like Everyday Grammar and Words and Their Stories. It remains free with no registration and the site was still publishing daily in August 2026. The register is deliberately simplified American English, so it builds comprehension rather than natural idiom, and its YouTube channel has been dormant since 2025 — go to the website rather than the video feed.

[Website](https://learningenglish.voanews.com/) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@voalearningenglish)

### All Ears English

*Daily American English podcast about connection, idioms and small talk.*

Lindsay and Michelle release episodes every weekday — it was still publishing daily in August 2026 — focusing on natural conversational American English, idioms and the social side of speaking, with a deliberately upbeat 'connection not perfection' stance. ### Breaking News English

*Every news story rewritten at seven levels, with audio and worksheets.*

website, media · A2–C1 · Free · reading, listening, vocabulary

Sean Banville publishes current news stories graded into seven versions from very easy to near-native, each with audio at several speeds and a stack of downloadable exercises — gap-fills, dictations, discussion questions. The multi-level format means you can read the same story at your level and then reread it a level up, which is an unusually effective way to push comprehension. It is entirely free, but the site is ad-supported and visually cluttered, and the worksheets are aimed at classrooms so self-studiers will ignore half of them.

[Website](https://breakingnewsenglish.com/)

### ELSA Speak

*Speech-recognition app that scores your pronunciation sound by sound.*

app · A1–C1 · Freemium · pronunciation, speaking

You read prompts aloud and ELSA marks which individual phonemes were off, showing what to do differently, plus AI role-plays for conversation practice — the closest thing to a pronunciation coach that runs on your phone at any hour. Most useful if you have a specific accent problem you already know about and want targeted, repeatable drilling. The free tier is limited and the app pushes hard towards Pro; scoring is calibrated to American English and can be harsh or plain wrong on perfectly intelligible non-standard pronunciation, so do not let it grind down your confidence.

[Website](https://elsaspeak.com/) · [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/elsa-speak-english-learning/id1083804886) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@ELSAspeakglobal) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=us.nobarriers.elsa)

### engVid

*Over 2,300 free whiteboard lessons from nine teachers, with quizzes.*

youtube, website, course · A1–C1 · Free · grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, writing

The classic free English video library: nine long-serving teachers with different accents and styles cover grammar points, vocabulary sets, business English, IELTS and TOEFL, each lesson followed by a quiz, and new lessons are still going up in 2026. Because the teachers overlap, you can find the same topic explained four different ways until one clicks. Presentation is old-fashioned whiteboard-and-marker with no learning path, so you must know what you want to study — it is a reference shelf, not a course.

[Website](https://www.engvid.com/)

### IELTS Liz

A former IELTS examiner's archive of over 300 pages covering every part of the test, with model answers, band descriptors explained, and the specific mistakes that cap people at band 6 — the Writing Task 2 material in particular is better than most paid courses. It is IELTS-specific rather than general English, the site is organised as a blog archive that takes patience to navigate, and the advanced lessons and ebooks are paid.

Luke Thompson is a teacher and stand-up comedian, and his podcast reached episode 1,000 in August 2026 with a back catalogue of rambling conversations, comedy, interviews and language analysis in natural British English. ### mmmEnglish

*Australian teacher drilling the patterns that get intermediate learners unstuck.*

youtube, course · B1–B2 · Freemium · speaking, grammar, pronunciation

Emma teaches word patterns, prepositions, and imitation-based speaking practice where you repeat after her to work on rhythm and connected speech, aimed squarely at intermediate learners who understand a lot but freeze when speaking. Courses are paid and hosted off-site, the accent model is Australian, and beginners will find the assumed baseline too high.

### Oxford Bookworms Library

*Over 270 stories rewritten at seven levels, so you can read whole books early.*

book · A1–C1 · Paid · reading, vocabulary

Graded readers are the highest-return, least-used tool in English learning: these are novels, thrillers, classics and non-fiction rewritten within a controlled vocabulary for each of seven stages from A1 to C1, most with audio, so you can finish real books long before you could read the originals. Reading several at a comfortable level does more for fluency than grinding through one text that is too hard. They are paid, priced per title, and the simplifications strip out much of the original style — the point is volume of comprehensible reading, not literary appreciation.

[Website](https://elt.oup.com/catalogue/items/global/graded_readers/oxford_bookworms_library/)

### r/EnglishLearning

*Large subreddit where natives answer 'is this sentence correct?' within minutes.*

community · Free · grammar, vocabulary, writing

The most active free place to get a specific question answered — why one preposition and not another, whether a sentence sounds natural, what a piece of slang means — usually with several native speakers weighing in and disagreeing productively about register and dialect. Also a good barometer of which resources learners actually stick with. Answers come from ordinary speakers rather than teachers, so explanations of why can be wrong even when the correction is right, and it works best as a supplement to structured study.

[Website](https://www.reddit.com/r/EnglishLearning/)

### Rachel's English

*The reference channel for American pronunciation, rhythm and reductions.*

youtube, course, podcast · B1–C2 · Freemium · pronunciation, speaking, listening

Over a thousand free videos analysing American English at the level of individual sounds, linking, stress and the reductions ('gonna', 'wanna', swallowed T's) that make natives hard to understand, often using slow-motion mouth close-ups and clips from films. ### Speak English With Vanessa

youtube, course · A2–C1 · Freemium · speaking, listening, vocabulary

### The Britannica Dictionary

*American learner's dictionary with more example sentences than any other.*

dictionary · A2–C2 · Free · vocabulary, writing, reading

This is the former Merriam-Webster Learner's Dictionary under Britannica's roof (learnersdictionary.com now redirects here), and its distinguishing feature is volume of examples — most entries show the word in many real sentences, which teaches usage far better than a definition does. Best for learners of American English who keep producing grammatically correct but unnatural sentences. It is American-only in spelling and idiom, the site is ad-supported, and the rebrand means older bookmarks and links point at the wrong place.

[Website](https://www.britannica.com/dictionary)

### Vocabulary.com

An adaptive quiz engine that keeps testing you on words in varied contexts until they stick, backed by dictionary entries written as short essays about how a word is really used rather than terse definitions. ### COCA (English-Corpora.org)

*Search a billion words of real American English to settle usage questions.*

tool · B2–C2 · Free · writing, vocabulary, grammar

The Corpus of Contemporary American English lets you ask which words actually collocate with which, whether a phrasing is common in academic writing but rare in speech, and how usage has shifted over decades — evidence rather than opinion, which is exactly what advanced writers need. Free after a quick registration, with sister corpora for British English, news and academic text. The interface is academic and unfriendly, free accounts hit query limits, and it assumes you can interpret concordance lines, so it is genuinely a C-level tool.

[Website](https://www.english-corpora.org/coca/)

### Culips English Podcast

*Natural conversations dissected phrase by phrase, with detailed study guides.*

podcast · B1–C1 · Paid · listening, vocabulary, speaking

Over a thousand lessons in which hosts from Canada, the US and the UK hold a natural conversation and then unpack the idioms, slang and connected speech in it, with study guides, interactive transcripts and a Discord community. The explain-the-real-conversation format is the most efficient way to convert listening into usable phrases. The archive is largely behind a membership at about $15 a month or $105 a year, only sample guides are free, and the level is stated as intermediate and up, so beginners should look elsewhere first.

[Website](https://www.culips.com/) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/culips-everyday-english-podcast/id279999557) · [RSS](https://esl.culips.com/feed/podcast/)

### Elllo

The English Listening Lesson Library Online holds several thousand unscripted conversations recorded with speakers from all over the world, each with transcript, vocabulary notes, a quiz and a difficulty rating. ### Ludwig

*Check whether your sentence sounds English by finding it in published texts.*

tool · B1–C2 · Freemium · writing, vocabulary

Type a phrase you are unsure about and Ludwig shows you real sentences from newspapers, journals and books that use it, so you can see whether native writers actually say 'discuss about' or 'make a research'. It answers the question a dictionary cannot — not what a word means but whether your combination of words exists. The free tier limits daily searches and holds back some features, results skew towards formal written English rather than speech, and it will confirm a phrasing exists without telling you whether it fits your context.

[Website](https://ludwig.guru/)

### Perfect English Grammar

*Clear grammar explanations with free exercises, written by one teacher.*

website · A1–C1 · Free · grammar, writing

Seonaid Beckwith explains one grammar point per page in plain language with example sentences and several free practice exercises, covering tenses, conditionals, reported speech, articles and the rest of the usual trouble spots. When you need a fast, trustworthy answer to 'when do I use the present perfect', this is quicker than any video. Explanations are British-oriented and deliberately simplified, so advanced learners chasing nuance will outgrow it and should move to a full reference grammar; some downloadable packs are paid.

[Website](https://www.perfect-english-grammar.com/)

Twice a week, one world-news story explained at deliberately reduced speed, followed by an English expression taught in context — the format that gets intermediate learners listening to real content without hitting a wall. ### Randall's ESL Cyber Listening Lab

*Graded listening quizzes on everyday situations, free since the 1990s.*

website, tool · A2–C1 · Free · listening, vocabulary

Randall Davis has been recording scripted but natural dialogues — booking a hotel, a doctor's appointment, a job interview — with pre-listening vocabulary, comprehension quizzes and full scripts, organised into easy, medium and difficult tiers. ### Sounds of Speech (University of Iowa)

*Animated cutaway diagrams showing how your mouth makes each English sound.*

tool, website · Free · pronunciation, listening

For every English consonant and vowel you get an animation of the tongue, lips and vocal folds, a written description of the articulation and video of a speaker producing it — the visual explanation that finally fixes sounds like /θ/, /ɹ/ and the vowel pairs no amount of listening had corrected. Free, from the University of Iowa, no account needed. It is a phonetics reference rather than a practice tool: there is no recording, feedback or drilling, and it covers American English (with separate Spanish and German sections).

[Website](https://soundsofspeech.uiowa.edu/)

### The Accent's Way (Hadar Shemesh)

*Pronunciation and confidence coaching from a non-native speaker's perspective.*

youtube, podcast, course · B1–C2 · Freemium · pronunciation, speaking

Hadar Shemesh is a non-native speaker who teaches clarity rather than accent erasure — her framing is that you do not have to sound American to sound good — through videos, a podcast, a free pronunciation masterclass, a 14-day plan and a list of commonly mispronounced words. That perspective makes her unusually good on the psychological side of speaking up in a second language. The substantial courses are paid, the emphasis is on American sounds despite the framing, and the free material is spread across several formats rather than one path.

[Website](https://hadarshemesh.com/) · [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/show/5ol9UbO7nDaoO7V2pPYzmP) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/il/podcast/in-fluency/id1492946397)

### Write & Improve (Cambridge)

*Submit writing, get instant CEFR-level feedback and marked-up corrections.*

tool · A1–C1 · Freemium · writing, grammar

Cambridge's own automated marking tool: write to one of hundreds of prompts, and within seconds you get an estimated CEFR level plus sentence-by-sentence highlights of what needs fixing, then resubmit and watch the score move. For a self-studying learner with no teacher, this closes the biggest gap in solo English study — nobody to correct your writing. The feedback is automated, so it flags problems more reliably than it explains them, and richer features and exam-style tasks sit behind the paid tier.

[Website](https://writeandimprove.com/)
