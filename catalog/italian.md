### Easy Italian (YouTube)

*Street interviews with real Italians, dual Italian/English subtitles on every clip.*

youtube · A1–C1 · Freemium · listening, vocabulary, immersion

The Italian arm of the Easy Languages project, run by Katie and Matteo out of Milan. Unscripted interviews give you the speed, filler words and regional accents textbooks hide, and the 'Super Easy Italian' series slows everything down for beginners. Videos are free with dual subtitles; the membership adds interactive transcripts and exercises. The main series is genuinely hard at A1 — use Super Easy Italian first.

[Website](https://www.youtube.com/@EasyItalian)

### Learn Italian with Lucrezia

*Lucrezia Oddone's calm, clearly-spoken grammar lessons and Rome vlogs.*

youtube · A1–B2 · Freemium · listening, grammar, vocabulary

A DITALS-certified teacher who has been publishing since 2013 and now has one of the largest Italian-learning channels. The strength is her diction: she speaks slowly and precisely in Italian with subtitles, so the vlogs double as listening practice while the lesson videos untangle things like ci/ne and the congiuntivo. Best for A2–B1 learners who want structured grammar without leaving Italian. YouTube is free; her courses and ebooks are paid.

[Website](https://www.youtube.com/@lucreziaoddone)

### Podcast Italiano

*Davide Gemello's all-Italian podcast on culture, language and ideas, with transcripts.*

podcast, youtube, course · B1–C1 · Freemium · listening, vocabulary, immersion

Running since 2016 and the reference point for intermediate Italian listening — monologues and interviews entirely in Italian, with free transcripts and a separate 'Principiante' feed for A2 learners. Davide is a linguistics nerd, so episodes often dig into etymology, dialects and register rather than just tourist topics. Free on all podcast apps and YouTube; the Club (Patreon) and courses are paid. Genuinely hard below B1 — start with the Principiante feed.

[Website](https://www.podcastitaliano.com/) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/podcast-italiano-learn-italian-intermediate-advanced/id1163599279) · [RSS](https://rss.buzzsprout.com/2413795.rss) · [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/show/1y4WrXQPfvoBCyWZBx5vFi) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@PodcastItaliano)

### Vocabolario Treccani

*Italy's most authoritative monolingual dictionary, free online with etymologies.*

dictionary · B1–C2 · Free · vocabulary, reading

The reference Italians themselves reach for: full definitions, etymology, usage labels and a synonyms section, alongside Treccani's encyclopedia. Switching to a monolingual dictionary is one of the biggest jumps you can make at B1+, and this is the one to switch to. Entries are dense and written in fairly formal Italian, so keep a bilingual dictionary around for a while; some extras and the ad-free app are paid.

[Website](https://www.treccani.it/vocabolario/)

### Accademia della Crusca

*Italy's language academy answering usage questions — the final word on 'is this correct?'*

website · B2–C2 · Free · grammar, reading, writing

The consulenza linguistica archive holds hundreds of expert answers to exactly the questions advanced learners get stuck on: which auxiliary, whether a form is regional, whether a new anglicism is acceptable. Everything is in Italian and written for an educated native audience, so it is a C1-ish read. Free, and you can submit your own question, though only recurring or generally interesting ones get answered.

[Website](https://accademiadellacrusca.it/)

### Alessandro Barbero — We Are History

*Italy's most listenable historian lecturing at length — free advanced listening.*

youtube, media · B2–C2 · Free · listening, vocabulary, immersion

Barbero's lectures are a cult favourite among Italians and an accidental gift to learners: he speaks slowly, articulates unusually clearly, and structures arguments so you can follow the thread even when individual words escape you. Hours of free material on everything from Waterloo to medieval daily life. Nothing is adapted for learners — no subtitles you can rely on, and the vocabulary is educated and specialised.

[Website](https://www.youtube.com/@vassallidibarbero)

### CILS certification (Università per Stranieri di Siena)

*Italy's state-recognised proficiency exam, A1 to C2, plus the B1 cittadinanza level.*

website · A1–C2 · Paid · listening, reading, writing, speaking

Run by the University for Foreigners of Siena and recognised by the Italian foreign ministry: B2 admits you to Italian universities and the special B1 cittadinanza is the level required for citizenship by marriage. The site carries the official exam calendar, centre list and past papers, which are the most useful free study material here. Fees and sitting dates vary by centre, and results take two to four months.

[Website](https://cils.unistrasi.it/)

### News in Slow Italian

*Weekly world news read at reduced speed, with parallel beginner/intermediate/advanced feeds.*

podcast, course · A1–C1 · Freemium · listening, vocabulary, grammar

Three separate difficulty streams plus grammar and idiom segments and original fiction series, all rebuilt weekly around real news. A limited free episode is published each week; full archives, transcripts and exercises need a subscription, and the price is on the high side.

### Nuovo Espresso (ALMA Edizioni)

*The standard Italian-language classroom coursebook, six volumes A1 to C2.*

book, course · A1–C2 · Paid · grammar, reading, listening, writing

If you take a course in Italy or at an Italian cultural institute, this is very likely the book on the desk: communicative units, audio, and a workbook in one volume per level, with an access code for the digital edition. The main English-language community for Italian learners: grammar questions, resource comparisons, corrections of your writing, and regular threads from natives willing to explain regional usage. ### Short Stories in Italian for Beginners

*Eight genre stories written to A2–B1 with glossaries and comprehension questions.*

book · A2–B1 · Paid · reading, vocabulary

Olly Richards' graded readers solve the classic beginner problem that real Italian books are far too hard: sci-fi, crime and fantasy plots written within a controlled vocabulary, with per-chapter word lists and summaries so you rarely need a dictionary. ### Grammatica pratica della lingua italiana (Nocchi)

*The 'Nocchi' — compact rule cards plus graded exercises, A1 to B2, with answer key.*

book · A1–B2 · Paid · grammar, writing

The most widely used practice grammar in Italian-as-a-foreign-language teaching: one page of rule, several pages of graded exercises, tests and games, with solutions included so it works for self-study. The updated edition adds online audio. It is a workbook, not a reference tome — for exhaustive explanations of subjunctive nuance you'll want a fuller grammar, and the B1–C1 companion is a separate volume.

[Website](https://www.almaedizioni.it/it/catalogo/scheda/grammatica-pratica-edizione-aggiornata/)

### Il Coniugatore

*Type any Italian verb, get every tense and mood — including the ones you dread.*

tool · A1–C2 · Free · grammar, writing

A no-nonsense free conjugator covering the full paradigm including congiuntivo, condizionale, passato remoto and the compound tenses, with reflexive and negative forms. Handy precisely because Italian irregulars cluster in the tenses you meet late. Ad-supported and plain-looking, and like any conjugator it tells you the form but not when to use it — pair it with a grammar.

[Website](https://www.ilconiugatore.com/)

### Il Nuovo De Mauro

*Tullio De Mauro's dictionary, free on Internazionale — with frequency labels.*

dictionary · B1–C2 · Free · vocabulary, reading

About 160,000 entries, and uniquely useful for learners because De Mauro tags words by frequency band (fondamentale, di alto uso, comune…), so you can see at a glance whether a word is worth learning. Definitions are plainer than Treccani's, which makes it the friendlier first monolingual dictionary. Completely free and ad-supported; the interface is dated and there is no app.

[Website](https://dizionario.internazionale.it/)
