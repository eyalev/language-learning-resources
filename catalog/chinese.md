### HelloChinese

*The best gamified beginner app for Chinese specifically — tones and characters built in.*

app, course · A1–B1 · Freemium · vocabulary, grammar, listening, speaking

Designed for Mandarin rather than retrofitted from a European-language template, so it drills tones with speech recognition, teaches stroke order for handwriting, and lets you toggle pinyin off as you improve. The free tier covers a real beginner course; premium unlocks later units, immersion videos and extra review. It plateaus around HSK 3–4 — treat it as your first six months, not your whole plan.

[Website](https://www.hellochinese.cc/) · [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hellochinese-learn-chinese/id1001507516) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.hellochinese) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@HelloChineseApp)

### Pleco

*The Chinese dictionary every serious learner installs first, and never uninstalls.*

dictionary, app · A1–C2 · Freemium · reading, vocabulary

A free, offline-capable Chinese-English dictionary with stroke-order animations, handwriting and audio search, plus paid add-ons for OCR (point your camera at a menu or sign), document reading and extra dictionaries. The free version alone is better than any competitor; the paid bundles are one-time purchases rather than a subscription. Its flashcard system is powerful but fiddly — many people pair Pleco for lookup with a separate SRS. Simplified and traditional both supported.

[Website](https://www.pleco.com/) · [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pleco-chinese-dictionary/id341922306) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pleco.chinesesystem)

### Chinese Grammar Wiki (AllSet Learning)

*Every Mandarin grammar point, sorted by level, with plenty of example sentences.*

website · A1–C1 · Free · grammar

The reference to bookmark: each structure gets a short explanation, a formula and a stack of natural example sentences, organised A1 through C1 so you can both look things up and work through a level systematically. Free on the web with an optional paid ebook version. It is a reference, not a course — it explains what a structure means but gives you no drilling or production practice.

[Website](https://resources.allsetlearning.com/chinese/grammar/Main_Page)

### Chinese Proficiency Test (HSK) — Official Site

*Official HSK and HSKK registration, test dates, syllabuses and sample papers.*

website · A1–C2 · Free · reading, listening, writing

The authoritative source for what each HSK level actually requires, where to sit the exam, and free official practice papers — worth checking directly rather than relying on third-party summaries, since the HSK 3.0 standard changed level boundaries and vocabulary counts substantially. Registration and information are free; the exam itself has a fee. The site is functional but clunky, and English translations lag the Chinese pages.

[Website](https://www.chinesetest.cn/)

### ChinesePod

*A vast back catalogue of dialogue-based audio lessons from Newbie to Advanced.*

podcast, course · A1–C1 · Freemium · listening, vocabulary, grammar

Two decades of lessons built around a short natural dialogue plus bilingual discussion, graded across six levels — the Intermediate and Upper Intermediate tiers in particular are still among the best listening material for Mandarin. Many episodes are free through podcast feeds; transcripts and exercises need a subscription. Caveat: the platform is essentially in maintenance mode, with little new content and slow support, so treat it as an archive rather than a living course.

[Website](https://www.chinesepod.com/) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/chinesepod-advanced/id1529838325) · [RSS](https://anchor.fm/s/317e2da0/podcast/rss) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=llc.chinesepod.android)

### Du Chinese

*Graded Chinese stories with native audio, tap-for-pinyin and a built-in dictionary.*

app, website · A1–C1 · Freemium · reading, listening, vocabulary

Thousands of short lessons across six difficulty tiers, each with clear recorded audio, a pop-up dictionary and toggleable pinyin, so you can read comfortably from your first hundred characters. The stories are written for learners rather than native content, which is exactly what makes it work early on but also why you should graduate to real material later.

### Hacking Chinese

*Fifteen years of practical, evidence-minded articles on how to actually study Mandarin.*

website, podcast · Free · listening, reading, vocabulary, pronunciation

Olle Linge writes long, careful pieces on tone learning, character memorisation, listening strategy and dealing with plateaus, plus regularly updated best-of lists for podcasts, YouTube channels and reading material. There is an accompanying podcast and free challenge platform. It is about method rather than content — you still need a course, a dictionary and input, but this tells you how to use them well.

[Website](https://www.hackingchinese.com/) · [Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/hacking-chinese-podcast/id1536284827) · [RSS](https://anchor.fm/s/388eb9e8/podcast/rss) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@HackingChinese) · [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/show/5iCRv1jg3j3yJZGJlYVYaO)

### Integrated Chinese (Cheng & Tsui)

*The standard university Mandarin textbook series, now in its fourth edition.*

book, course · A1–B2 · Paid · reading, writing, grammar, vocabulary

Four volumes of systematic grammar, dialogues and character work, used by most North American college programmes, with workbooks, audio and a web app for exercises. If you want a spine for self-study with a clear sequence and available answer keys, this is the safest choice. It is expensive, the dialogues are dated and campus-centric, and it is written for classroom use — self-learners need to supply their own speaking practice.

[Website](https://www.cheng-tsui.com/browse/integrated-chinese)

### Mandarin Companion Graded Readers

*Full novellas retold within 150, 300 or 450 characters — your first real book in Chinese.*

book · A1–B1 · Paid · reading, vocabulary

Classic stories (Sherlock Holmes, Jane Eyre, Chinese folk tales) rewritten with strictly controlled character sets and reset in Chinese settings, so a beginner can finish an entire book without constant lookups. Finishing one is a genuine motivation milestone that app-based reading rarely delivers. Books are cheap but paid, available in simplified and traditional, and the Level 0/1 range is narrow — you outgrow the series around HSK 4.

[Website](https://mandarincompanion.com/)

### MDBG Chinese Dictionary

*Free browser dictionary built on CC-CEDICT, with character decomposition and stroke order.*

dictionary, website · Free · reading, vocabulary, writing

The default desktop lookup for most learners: search by pinyin, character, English or handwriting, then drill into components, stroke order and example words. It also has a bulk text annotation tool that adds pinyin above a whole passage, which is handy for prepping reading material. Plain and ad-light, but purely a reference — no lessons, no spaced repetition.

[Website](https://www.mdbg.net/chinese/dictionary)

### Skritter

*Handwriting SRS that grades every stroke as you write characters from memory.*

app, tool · A1–C1 · Paid · writing, reading, vocabulary

You write characters with a finger or stylus and it marks stroke order and shape in real time, scheduling reviews so you actually retain them; it ships with HSK, Integrated Chinese and other textbook lists. Only Chinese and Japanese are supported, which is why it is here rather than in the general tools. Roughly $15/month or $100/year after a 7-day trial, and it is worth it only if handwriting is genuinely one of your goals — many learners reasonably skip production of characters entirely.

[Website](https://skritter.com/) · [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/skritter-write-chinese/id1370892114) · [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCjgfULWh8BVpW4kebqEr91w) · [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.inkren.skritter.chinese)

### Yoyo Chinese

### Yoyo Chinese Interactive Pinyin Chart

*Every pinyin syllable in all four tones, with native audio and video explanations.*

tool, website · A1 · Free · pronunciation, listening

A free chart where you click any initial-final combination to hear it in each tone, with short video lessons on the sounds English speakers get wrong (zh/ch/sh vs j/q/x, the ü vowel, the third-tone sandhi). Working through this before you memorise vocabulary saves months of fossilised pronunciation errors. It is a reference tool, not a course, so pair it with feedback from a teacher or tutor.

[Website](https://www.yoyochinese.com/chinese-learning-tools/Mandarin-Chinese-pronunciation-lesson/pinyin-chart-table)

Bridges the gap between beginner input channels and native content: real subjects covered in natural but carefully paced Chinese, staying in the target language throughout. ### Chinese Forums

A long-running forum where advanced learners, translators and teachers review resources, discuss study methods and run reading groups — the signal-to-noise ratio is far better than social media. Searching the archive answers most 'which textbook / how do I get past HSK 4' questions immediately. ### Chinese Zero To Hero

*Structured video courses following the HSK syllabus all the way to HSK 6.*

course · A1–C1 · Paid · grammar, vocabulary, reading, listening

The most complete HSK-aligned course track available online, with grammar explanations, vocabulary drilling and mock exams for each level, taught clearly in English with Chinese examples. If your goal is the HSK certificate for study or work in China, this is the straightest path. Courses are paid per level (a free YouTube channel previews the teaching style), and the HSK-exam focus means less everyday conversational practice.

[Website](https://www.chinesezerotohero.com/)

### Dashu Mandarin 大叔中文

Unscripted multi-speaker conversation is the hardest listening skill and this is the best learner-oriented source of it: real interruptions, opinions and slang, but from teachers who keep things intelligible. ### Dong Chinese

*Learn characters through real etymology plus frequency-ordered reading practice.*

app, website, tool · A1–B2 · Freemium · reading, vocabulary, writing

Shows you where characters actually come from — ancient forms, sound components, meaning components — instead of inventing mnemonics, then feeds you sentences and short media graded to what you already know. The character-origin data is genuinely scholarly and the free tier is generous. The interface is dense and does not hold your hand, so it suits self-directed learners more than beginners who want a guided path.

### Grace Mandarin Chinese

*Clear, well-produced videos on tones, pronunciation and how Mandarin actually sounds.*

A Taiwanese teacher whose channel is unusually strong on the things textbooks gloss over: tone pairs, tone changes in fast speech, mouth position for tricky sounds, and the difference between mainland and Taiwanese pronunciation. ### Hack Chinese

*Chinese-specific spaced repetition with ready-made HSK and textbook word lists.*

tool, website · A1–C1 · Paid · vocabulary, reading

A vocabulary SRS built only for Mandarin: import HSK levels, Integrated Chinese chapters or your own lists, and it schedules reviews with sensible defaults instead of Anki's endless configuration. Good for people who want spaced repetition to just work. It is a paid subscription doing something Anki does free, and like all SRS it teaches recognition, not usage — pair it with reading.

### Heavenly Path 飞升宝典

*Community-built roadmap from graded readers to native novels, games and shows.*

website, community · B1–C2 · Free · reading, immersion

A free Notion wiki that solves 'what do I read next': hundreds of native books, webnovels, manhua, games and TV shows sorted by real difficulty, with a step-by-step guide out of textbook Chinese and into native material. It is the single best answer for intermediate learners stuck in the HSK plateau. Notion's interface is clunky on phones, and recommendations skew towards webnovels and genre fiction.

### Lazy Chinese

*Slow, all-Chinese storytelling with gestures and props you can follow from day one.*

youtube · A1–A2 · Free · listening, immersion, vocabulary

Suqing tells simple stories entirely in Mandarin, using drawings, objects and clear enunciation so meaning arrives from context instead of translation — the comprehensible-input approach applied properly to Chinese. It is the standard first listening resource for beginners who want their ear trained early. No English explanations means you need patience at the very start, and the videos build vocabulary slowly by design.

[Website](https://www.youtube.com/@Lazy-Chinese) · [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/show/7cbdgvtWWMOwl4eejMRPWI)

### Mandarin Blueprint

*Character-first course built on the Hanzi Movie Method mnemonic system.*

course · A1–B2 · Paid · reading, vocabulary, pronunciation

A long, structured course whose distinguishing idea is the Hanzi Movie Method: you assign actors, sets and props to sound and tone components, then build vivid scenes to lock in thousands of characters. Learners who bounce off rote character study often find it works where nothing else did. It is expensive (hundreds of dollars, sold as subscription or lifetime), the setup phase is heavy before you see returns, and the mnemonic system is not for everyone.

[Website](https://www.mandarinblueprint.com/)

### Mandarin Click

### Outlier Linguistics Chinese Character Dictionary

*Palaeography-based character add-on for Pleco: what each component really contributes.*

dictionary, tool · Paid · reading, vocabulary

Built by linguists working from excavated bronze and oracle-bone forms, it explains for each character which parts carry sound and which carry meaning, correcting a lot of folk etymology that circulates in learner circles. ### 萌典 MoeDict

The best free reference for traditional characters and Taiwanese Mandarin usage, built on the Ministry of Education's official dictionaries and showing zhuyin (bopomofo) alongside pinyin.
